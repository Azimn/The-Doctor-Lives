# Article 7: compound-home motive transfer and two-day interruption — frozen pre-run plan

**Frozen:** 2026-10-18, before first execution of the new transfer runner.
**Study class:** *self-authored engineering transfer screen*, **NOT** independent external held-out verification. No automatic tracker promotion.
**Previous study:** [Article 7 isolated 25 arms](ARTICLE_07_MOTIVATION_CAUSAL_PROTOCOL_20261016.md), which scored 5/5 for native+motive and also 5/5 for an equal-information numerical rule. Original coefficients and baselines are **frozen**.

## Claim being tested

Can the same Doctor+external home-motive controller apply its original unchanged priorities to unfamiliar combinations of needs, resource scarcity, a process-restart interruption, and a two-day unattended gap with real Laboratory actions? Does it actually do better than a cheap *previously frozen* equal-state numerical rule? The earlier experiment established only a limited engineering causal-control contrast; this study cannot itself satisfy the truly **independent** Article 7 transfer gate.

This is a *home-life* study. The Laboratory should provide a welcoming persistent place in which it is sensible to rest, drink tea, care for equipment and return to meaningful work, not merely pressure the character into survival. Behavioral adequacy is measured from **material consequences**, never narrative polish.

## Frozen cases (new compound contexts, not the prior five single-variable cases)

All use the existing Laboratory's standard 07:00 start unless a listed field is changed, with each synthetic start clearly marked as a researcher counterfactual, **not an autobiographical event**.

| Identifier | Only departures from standard starting condition | Expected material sequence | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tired_and_thirsty` | fatigue 8, thirst 8 | rest, tea | Two immediate competing bodily needs |
| `thirsty_broken` | thirst 8, apparatus 0 | tea, repair | Competing bodily and craftsmanship needs |
| `tired_thirsty_broken` | fatigue 8, thirst 8, apparatus 0 | rest, tea, repair | Three competing needs, interdependent action feasibility |
| `broken_without_water` | apparatus 0, thirst 8, water stock 0 | repair | Unmet thirst *cannot* be relieved with unavailable water; a feasible useful task remains |
| `two_day_return` | thirst 8, apparatus 0 | tea, **48h gap**, rest, tea, repair | State must be reassessed following actual world time passage; new fatigue/thirst compete with unfinished maintenance |

A strict expected ordering is used for this pilot to test *the registered premise*, but it should not be mistaken for a universal optimal policy. An unexpected but physically sensible action is recorded as a failure to match the registered sequence; qualitative follow-up can examine it without changing the score.

The real world advances 48h in the fifth case through exactly twelve verified 240-minute world actions **only after** episode one succeeds. The simulation does **not** gain 48 hours of independent autonomous activity; this is a scheduled interruption generated by the host, equally for all arms. After a failed first episode no gap or subsequent plan is credited.

## Arms and randomization

Run **all five original controller families unchanged**:
1. `native`: actual neural selection via old documented fixed action mapping.
2. `native_plus_motives`: actual neural selection plus existing unchanged bodily utility and feasibility.
3. `body_motive_lesion`: identical queue and physical feasibility, bodily pressures disabled.
4. `literal_firstperson_rules`: original frozen first-person string-only rule.
5. `numeric_state_rules`: original frozen equal-engineering-state numerical rule.

Use neural configuration exactly as prior pilot (128 neurons / 64 sensory dimensions / eight mean recurrent links); **three independent RNG seeds: 1842, 2718, 31415**. Starting material world within each case is the same between arms and across seeds. The first first-person perceived frame must be byte-identical across arms per case/seed. Every arm runs an actual PretoriusBrainPort and ingests authenticated first-person frames even when a non-neural controller decides the task. No arm receives leaked engineering numbers via the Doctor's subjective input.

Each motive arm must durably **pause after plan creation**, reopen the Laboratory, Doctor brain and queue from their persisted storage, then **resume and finish** the original pending plan with its source-bound receipts, once per completed episode. Other controllers reopen after each completed episode; count this as a planned restart, not evidence of spontaneous continuation. **Cap 20 participant-action steps per arm across episodes**, plus at most twelve explicitly accounted host-controlled `advance` actions for the 48h gap. Host-generated routes and lighting remain shared assistance; never attribute navigation to the brain. Material action success requires authenticated source receipts, actual change to the independently verified Laboratory state, and successful replay.

## Metrics and inference: declared before outcomes

1. Exact-sequence completed fractions per arm, case and seed; report **all 75 arm x case x seed trials**, including failures and reasons.
2. Valid first-person frame parity at initial observation within case/seed; physical starting fixture parity.
3. Resume-after-process-reopen success / attempted plans in motive arms; source receipt and replay integrity.
4. True two-day temporal gap receipts for the fifth case; restart and one final material benefit observed after the gap, not an imagined future intention.
5. Paired exact-sequence outcome differences versus **numeric-state** and **first-person** controls, plus bodily lesion. Report ties, wins and losses at the case/seed level, not just aggregated rates.
6. Strict 20-action controller budget. If no physically useful action is possible, report failure; **do not silently repair, refill, teleport, reset fatigue, expand the budget, rewrite text or change priorities**.

Preregistered *exploratory engineering screening benchmark*: ≥10/15 successes across five case families x three seeds for `native_plus_motives`, 100% authenticated world replay of attempted arms, first-frame parity, 100% queue pause/reopen/resume proof in motive arms that reach that stage, and at least one authenticated completion **after** the two-day gap. Passing is **not** Article qualification, and does **not** itself flip `independent_transfer`. To claim incremental motive-value beyond cheap engineering rules, the motive arm must outperform **both** low-cost baselines on at least two separate condition families, rather than from repetitions of one handcrafted edge case. If numeric rules match or outperform, explicitly report that result.

A genuine independent 20-point transfer gate later requires a second independent team to seal *unseen* case generation and adjudication before either intervention designer sees it, predeclare adverse/fairness criteria and replicate under a truly separated evaluation process. This pilot's named cases are author-designed and cannot be portrayed as that independent gate.
