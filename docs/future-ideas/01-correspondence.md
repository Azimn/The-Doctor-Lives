# Future Idea 01: Correspondence

**Status:** Proposed, deferred. Research note only; not a release requirement.  
**Recorded:** 2026-10-30  
**Project:** The Doctor Lives / Pretorius, with a possible future Frankenstein Village connection.

## Why consider it?

Pretorius aims to become a persistent, convincing artificial individual rather than a conventional companion responding only when addressed. The Articles of Artificiality motivate richer continuity, memory, relationships, agency, and participation in a world. Correspondence offers a gradual, historically plausible way to introduce social life without first requiring a fully populated village.

**Release priority comes first:** Get the Doctor running independently in a durable external simulation, with autonomous day-to-day activity. Do not let this proposal delay that milestone or become a prerequisite for shipping the current Doctor.

## Phase One: A postal channel in the Laboratory

Introduce a physical, in-world mail system. Pretorius can receive letters and written notes, read pamphlets and newsletters, compose replies, and choose to initiate correspondence himself. The operator can communicate with him through letters, as can a limited set of authored or simulated correspondents before Frankenstein Village is operational.

Communications should fit the simulation's nineteenth-century setting. Mail is asynchronous: a message is written, sent, transported, and delivered after an appropriate interval. A nearby handwritten note may arrive relatively quickly; correspondence from farther away should take longer. There is no modern instant-message channel disguised as post.

An item should have a sender, intended recipient, writing and delivery times, an in-world source or location, and a clear provenance record. The Doctor encounters the delivered artifact through ordinary Laboratory perception and reading, not through raw system metadata. He is free to read later, disregard a pamphlet, reply after reflection, or write without being prompted. His decisions, relationships, and recollections may be affected by what he actually receives and does.

## Agency is the governing principle

**Delivery is not attention; attention is not reading; reading is not consent to reply.** These are separate in-world possibilities. A letter can arrive without Pretorius noticing it, be noticed but left unopened, be opened and read without a response, or be answered much later or never. He may choose to initiate contact on his own terms. No outside sender, including the operator, is entitled to an answer.

His available time, interests, priorities, relationships, temperament, and current activity should shape these choices rather than an automatic inbox-processing rule. A lack of reply is a valid simulation outcome, not a task failure, though correspondents may reasonably react to it within the fiction. A delivery record may exist for engineering purposes, but his memory must reflect only what he actually perceived or did, not what silently entered a queue.

To evaluate this later, test unread letters during sustained work, letters read without response, voluntary delayed replies, and correspondence initiated without an operator prompt. Do not optimize success as messages answered or response speed; that would turn agency back into compliance.

## Phase One addendum: Parcels and physical deliveries

The postal channel should eventually carry **objects and packages**, not just written messages. A parcel is a delivered physical world object: it arrives through the same historically paced mail flow, can have packaging, markings, a sender, and contents, and can be opened, examined, stored, or ignored by Pretorius. Its contents must become available only through appropriate in-world interaction, rather than appearing directly in his inventory or cognition.

This would allow modest gifts, scientific materials, books, and unexpected artifacts to enter the Laboratory through a recognizable social mechanism. A future Phase Two communication instrument could even arrive as a mysterious parcel, linking the two phases naturally. The exact item logistics and constraints remain open for later design.

## Expansion path

Initially, authored letters, period-appropriate publications, and a small number of simulated correspondents can populate the postal stream. When Frankenstein Village has stable residents, routines, and a shared world timeline, its characters can send and receive correspondence through the same channel. Local notes and longer-distance post can coexist, with delivery represented as actual events in the shared simulation.

## What to evaluate later

Does delayed communication produce believable follow-through, selective attention, persistent social memory, voluntary contact, and relationships that develop across time? Can the Doctor track unanswered letters and respond according to his own circumstances, rather than simply answering every prompt? Tests should distinguish genuine ongoing behavioral consequences from a scripted exchange.

**Boundary:** This is a parked future direction, not an implementation instruction. The first milestone remains a running, persistent, autonomous Doctor.
