# Future Idea 02: The Interocitor (real-time correspondence)

**Status:** Exploratory, deferred. Concept only; not an implementation assignment or release criterion.  
**Recorded:** 2026-10-30  
**Depends on:** Phase One postal correspondence as a narrative introduction, and a functioning standalone Pretorius simulation as the actual project prerequisite.

## Premise

Phase Two would give Pretorius a way to **exchange text in real time** with an outside correspondent while remaining wholly inside his own first-person nineteenth-century Laboratory world. Unlike letters, the exchange is conversational and immediate. Unlike a conventional chatbot, he is interacting with a physical instrument in his environment, not receiving invisible system prompts.

The proposed inspiration is the **Interocitor**, a fantastic communication apparatus from the 1955 film *This Island Earth*, also featured in *Mystery Science Theater 3000: The Movie*. The name is provisional. Possible original alternatives include **Aetheric Telegraph**, **Electro-Telegram**, or **Correspondence Engine**. The historical setting is intentionally stretched by one strange scientific invention rather than modernized across the board.

## How it could enter the world

The apparatus, its plans, or an assembly kit could arrive **by parcel through Phase One's postal service**. Pretorius might choose to examine or assemble it, discover its purpose, and decide whether to use it. This is a proposed narrative route, not a scripted behavior or predetermined decision. The item's arrival should be a credible Laboratory event with persistent physical presence.

The instrument might display incoming characters on a luminous screen or print them on a narrow paper strip, with a keyboard or telegraph-like controls for replies. Its exact form, origin, operating principles, and aesthetic are deliberately unsettled. It can be visibly extraordinary without granting Pretorius knowledge of the underlying software.

## What Phase Two would enable

When the Doctor approaches and uses the device, he can hold a near-immediate, two-way **written conversation** with the operator or another approved remote correspondent. Telegram or another text service could be the outside-world endpoint. That service is transport infrastructure; inside the world, Pretorius perceives the apparatus and its messages, not Telegram, API calls, or internal implementation records.

He can initiate an exchange, reply, postpone responding, stop talking, or choose to do something else. Incoming contact should have an in-world arrival cue and should not seize his attention automatically. Unanswered messages can remain as durable artifacts or records he can revisit. His statements and memories should reflect what he actually said, received, observed, and decided.

The apparatus must not silently merge real-world participants with fabricated village residents. Each correspondent needs an internally coherent presented identity and an accurate underlying provenance record. Communication passes through the project's existing subject-native perception and memory boundaries, preserving first-person experience without falsifying the world's history.

## Agency: a conversation is an invitation, not a command

**Real-time delivery must not create a real-time response obligation.** The Interocitor is an optional object in Pretorius's world, not a privileged channel that interrupts or controls him. An incoming message may arrive without his noticing; he may see the machine signal and ignore it; he may read the words and choose not to answer. He may respond immediately, after a delay, at another time, or never. He can also initiate a conversation himself, end one, or leave the device unused while pursuing other commitments.

Separate message **arrival**, **in-world notification**, **perception**, **reading**, **decision**, and **outgoing reply**. External text transport must never mark an unread message as personally known, inject an obligatory conversational turn, or treat no response as a system error. An optional retained transcript may preserve delivery facts for the host, while Pretorius only recalls the portion he encountered through his subject-native experience.

When this proposal is eventually evaluated, deliberately interrupt tempting conversations with meaningful Laboratory work, allow messages to go unanswered, check that silence has no forced recovery script, and test voluntary re-engagement. Success means choices and continuity consistent with his circumstances, not maximum uptime, engagement, or reply rate.

## Voluntary appetite for conversation

The other side of the right to ignore the machine is the **freedom to seek it out**. Pretorius may enjoy a conversation, feel curious about a correspondent, wish to continue a prior discussion, or decide that talking to someone is more appealing than working alone. He could walk over to the Interocitor of his own accord, check whether someone is available, initiate a message, or return to an earlier exchange. His preferred frequency and style of contact may evolve as his experiences and relationships do.

This is a possibility, **not a prescribed social drive or an engagement objective**. The instrument should remain available as a genuine option among his other activities. There must be no mandatory check-in schedule, artificial craving, reply quota, or hidden optimization for message count or conversation duration. Conversely, self-initiated conversation should be possible without a fresh incoming message or operator prompt.

Future testing should examine whether he sometimes starts conversations for identifiable, context-dependent reasons, remembers what he intended to discuss, and can freely choose to stop or pursue another priority. Both spontaneous contact and voluntary silence count as valid outcomes.

## Scope and future possibilities

**Phase Two's deliverable, when approved, is text conversation only.** Audio could be explored as a later enhancement; moving images are an even more speculative possibility, not a target. This is not a requirement to add phone calls, video chat, modern computer interfaces, general Internet access, or new social simulation systems.

Questions reserved for later design include the apparatus's canonical name and appearance, its fictional explanation, how connection availability and time work, whether exchanges can involve Frankenstein Village inhabitants, and how to evaluate self-initiated conversation and genuine follow-through across sessions.

**Release boundary:** Do not implement this now or make it a blocker. The immediate priority is shipping a durable, autonomous Doctor operating in his external simulation. This document preserves a future direction without expanding current scope.
